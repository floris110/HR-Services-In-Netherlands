# Managing HR in the Netherlands Without Building a Full Department

When you run an international company in the Netherlands, HR quickly becomes part of your daily operations. Hiring needs to follow Dutch requirements. Employment contracts need to reflect local employment law. Payroll, sickness management, compliance, and employee support all need consistent attention.

The challenge is that these responsibilities do not always justify building a full in-house HR department. You may need regular support, but not necessarily a large internal team. You may also need help with one specific issue, such as:

* A dismissal procedure

* A compliance audit

* A 30% ruling

The practical approach is to set up HR support that matches the way your company operates. That means having access to Dutch HR knowledge while keeping responsibility clear for managers and employees.

## When You Need More Than Routine HR

Not every HR need is part of a regular schedule:

* **Sickness Management:** Requires careful follow-up under the Gatekeeper Improvement Act (*Wet verbetering poortwachter*).

* **Dismissal Procedures:** Require specific legal precision and attention.

* **Compliance Audits:** Help identify gaps in existing policies or employment contracts.

* **Training & Development:** Become increasingly critical as your workforce evolves.

HRHelp offers support for these specialized areas alongside its HR Teams service, including:

* **HR Settlers**

* **HR Reset**

* **HR S.O.S.**

These options are relevant when setting up initial HR processes, reviewing how your current approach works, or dealing with urgent HR matters.

This structure is useful as your needs change over time. You might start with setup assistance for a Netherlands operation, then expand to support HR transformation or an outsourced HR department. Alternatively, you may simply require focused assistance with a single Dutch HR matter rather than ongoing support.

For international companies, the main point is **local clarity**. Dutch employment contracts, payroll, sickness management, compliance, and dismissal procedures all sit within a specific legal and administrative context. Treating them as general administrative tasks can create avoidable confusion and risk.
