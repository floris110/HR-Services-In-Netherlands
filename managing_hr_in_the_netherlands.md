# Managing HR in the Netherlands Without Building a Full Department

When your company operates in the Netherlands, HR tasks quickly become part of your daily responsibilities. Hiring, payroll, employment contracts, sickness management, and compliance all require attention. Dutch employment law also brings its own terminology, procedures, and expectations, which can make routine decisions harder to manage from another country.

You may not need to build a complete in-house HR department to handle this work properly. You do need a clear point of responsibility, reliable processes, and support that understands the Dutch employment context.

## What HR Support Covers in the Netherlands

HR work does not stop when someone signs an employment contract. Areas requiring ongoing attention include:

* **HR Policies & Contracts:** Drafting compliant terms and keeping employee handbooks up to date.
* **Payroll Management:** Aligning salary adjustments, mandatory holiday allowances (minimum 8%), and pension obligations.
* **Sickness Management:** Navigating the strict 2-year reintegration requirements under the Dutch Gatekeeper Improvement Act (*Wet verbetering poortwachter*).
* **Training & Development:** Supporting ongoing employee growth and compliance requirements.
* **Dismissals & Compliance Audits:** Handling sensitive departures and internal reviews carefully, as mistakes under Dutch dismissal law can lead to costly court proceedings or settlement penalties.

### The 30% Ruling

The 30% ruling (*30%-regeling*) is another area where employers frequently require practical guidance. Because it directly intersects with payroll structures, salary thresholds, and tax withholding, it should be managed as an integrated part of the employee lifecycle rather than as an isolated administrative task.

> **Key Takeaway:** For international companies, the main challenge is knowing which Dutch process applies and when to take action. A local HR partner bridges this gap, keeping communication transparent across cross-border management and local teams.
